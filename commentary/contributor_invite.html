Iroko Commentaries · Contributor Invitation

Write from where your knowledge lives.

Iroko Commentaries publishes selected essays in the languages their authors know best, bringing scholarly, historical, practitioner, artistic, and community voices into a shared, preserved, and citable record.

```
```

The Iroko Historical Society invites scholars, practitioners, archivists, community historians, artists, elders, and independent researchers to contribute informed and carefully situated work to Iroko Commentaries.

We seek writing grounded in meaningful knowledge, sustained inquiry, lived experience, documentary evidence, artistic practice, lineage teaching, or community memory. Academic affiliation is welcomed but not required.

Contributors are encouraged to write in the language in which they can express their knowledge, experience, and argument most fully. English is not required for consideration or publication.

Current submission status

Commentaries are presently commissioned or accepted through editorial invitation.

Limited proposals may also be reviewed at the discretion of the editors. IHS does not guarantee a response, editorial review, or publication of unsolicited work.

01 · About the series

What is an Iroko Commentary?

An Iroko Commentary is a signed, edited essay that interprets, questions, documents, or reflects upon the histories, religions, cultures, archives, and living communities within the scope of the Iroko Historical Society.

A commentary may be scholarly, historical, personal, critical, archival, artistic, or practitioner-based. It may advance an argument, preserve a perspective, examine an artifact, reconsider an inherited interpretation, or place lived religious knowledge into conversation with the historical record.

Commentaries do not require the formal structure of a peer-reviewed journal article, but they should demonstrate intellectual seriousness, responsible representation, and a clear basis of knowledge.

A signed commentary represents the considered perspective of its author. It is not automatically an institutional statement, a final authority, or a declaration of universally accepted doctrine.

02 · Contributors

Who we publish

IHS welcomes writers whose work can deepen the historical and living record, including:

  • initiated practitioners and lineage authorities;
  • scholars and scholar-practitioners;
  • archivists, librarians, and public historians;
  • community historians and oral-history practitioners;
  • artists, musicians, and cultural workers;
  • elders, tradition bearers, and independent researchers;
  • writers whose knowledge has developed outside formal academic institutions.

Initiatory standing may be important to certain subjects, but no credential, office, degree, or title grants universal authority over every lineage, community, or tradition.

Contributors should identify the basis and limits of their perspective. This may include archival research, ethnographic fieldwork, oral testimony, lineage teaching, personal experience, artistic practice, legal analysis, historical study, or another clearly described foundation.

03 · Language and translation

The author’s language comes first

Contributors should write in the language in which they can express their knowledge, memory, experience, and argument most accurately. English is not the default language of authority, and fluency in English is not a condition of publication.

A commentary may be published in Spanish, French, Haitian Creole, Portuguese, Yoruba, English, or another language appropriate to the author and the work.

Authoritative edition

The original-language text

The version written or expressly approved by the author remains the authoritative edition.

Published translation

A reviewed language edition

A human-, author-, or editorially reviewed translation may be issued as a stable additional edition.

Reader access

Automatic translation dropdown

Individual pages may allow readers to request automatic translations in selected African, European, and Atlantic languages.

Translation status

Every version is identified

Each page should state which language is original, which versions have been reviewed, and which are temporary machine-generated reading aids.

Automatic translations are provided to widen access. They may contain errors, ambiguities, mistranslations, or losses of cultural, historical, and religious meaning. They are not published editions and do not replace the original text.

Where a word, title, prayer, office, lineage term, ritual concept, or culturally specific expression cannot be translated adequately, the original term may be retained and explained rather than flattened into an inaccurate equivalent.

A reviewed translation is an edition. An automatic dropdown translation is a reading aid.

04 · Publication relationship

One series, distinct voices

IHS-authored and guest commentaries appear within one unified publication series. Guest writers are not placed into a secondary or peripheral category. Their work forms part of the same intellectual conversation while remaining clearly attributed.

IHS Commentary

Work by an IHS author

A signed essay by an officer, curator, researcher, or contributor writing within the IHS project. Personal authorship remains distinct from an official institutional statement.

Guest Commentary

Work by an invited contributor

A signed essay by an outside author selected and edited for publication by IHS. The author retains ownership of the argument and does not speak on behalf of the Society.

Publication signifies that a work has been reviewed for relevance, clarity, ethical treatment, responsible sourcing, and consistency with the editorial purpose of the series.

Publication does not mean that IHS endorses every historical interpretation, theological position, political conclusion, lineage claim, translation choice, or opinion expressed by the author.

IHS may publish differing or competing interpretations when doing so contributes to a fuller and more responsible record.

05 · Editorial standards

What we look for

A strong commentary should do more than express a general opinion. It should offer readers a perspective worth preserving.

A clear contribution

The essay should present a discernible question, argument, interpretation, recollection, intervention, or documentary contribution.

Situated authority

Authors should explain, directly or through the substance of the essay, how they know what they know. Claims should be situated within an identifiable period, region, community, lineage, archive, body of evidence, or field of experience.

Responsible evidence

Historical and factual claims should be supported where appropriate. Citations may follow a formal scholarly style or a clear editorial format agreed upon with IHS.

Respect without avoidance

Commentaries may be critical, revisionist, or controversial. Respectful treatment does not require false consensus, avoidance of disagreement, or suppression of difficult questions.

Authorial voice

IHS values writing with a recognizable human voice. Contributors need not imitate academic prose or write in English, but the work should be deliberate, intelligible, and suited to permanent publication.

Translation transparency

Every translated version should identify whether it was produced automatically, reviewed by the author, edited by a human translator, or independently translated.

06 · Sacred and contested knowledge

Knowledge carries obligations

Contributors must exercise particular care when discussing sacred, restricted, private, initiatory, or community-controlled knowledge.

IHS will not knowingly publish material that:

  • reveals restricted ritual information without appropriate authority;
  • reproduces confidential oral histories, correspondence, or private records without authorization;
  • identifies private individuals without permission or a compelling historical justification;
  • exposes vulnerable people, communities, sacred sites, or active practices to foreseeable harm;
  • presents lineage-specific teachings as universally accepted doctrine;
  • makes serious allegations concerning living people without adequate evidence and appropriate editorial review;
  • converts sacred or culturally specific language into a misleading translation for the sake of apparent simplicity.

When traditions or authorities disagree, contributors should identify the community, lineage, historical period, region, or source from which a claim arises. Disagreement should be recorded accurately rather than erased in pursuit of an artificial consensus.

IHS reserves the right to decline, revise, delay, restrict, or remove material when publication would violate obligations to individuals, communities, archives, or living traditions.

07 · Copyright and preservation

The author retains the work

Contributors retain copyright in their original writing. Publication through IHS does not transfer authorship or ownership of the work to the Society.

By accepting publication, the author grants the Iroko Historical Society a nonexclusive, continuing license to publish, display, format, preserve, archive, prepare translations for access, and promote the accepted version through IHS websites, repositories, exhibits, newsletters, and related educational channels.

A machine-generated translation is treated as a separate reading aid. It is not an authoritative edition unless it is reviewed and expressly approved for publication.

Authors may republish their work elsewhere, provided that the original IHS publication is acknowledged and later revisions are clearly identified.

IHS will not materially alter an author’s argument without consultation. Substantive editorial changes require author approval. Minor corrections involving spelling, citation form, formatting, accessibility, links, and factual metadata may be made editorially.

Archival publication: Substantial Iroko Commentaries may be issued as professionally formatted archival editions and deposited in a trusted scholarly repository.

Where appropriate, the work may receive its own persistent identifier or DOI, with the author named as creator and the Iroko Historical Society identified as publisher.

The archival record should identify the original language, the authoritative edition, and any stable reviewed translations. Temporary dropdown translations need not be separately archived.

Photographs, artwork, archival documents, recordings, and other third-party materials must be accompanied by appropriate permissions or a documented legal basis for publication.

Unless otherwise agreed in writing, publication is unpaid.

08 · Editorial process

From proposal to publication

  1. Initial conversation. A contributor is invited to write or submits a brief proposal for editorial consideration.
  2. Language and scope. The author identifies the language in which they prefer to write, the proposed subject, and the basis of their knowledge.
  3. Editorial fit. IHS considers whether the proposed work serves the historical, cultural, scholarly, or community purpose of the series.
  4. Draft review. The editors review the manuscript for structure, clarity, evidence, ethical concerns, and treatment of sacred or contested material.
  5. Author revision. The author reviews requested changes and retains control over the substance of the argument.
  6. Edition planning. IHS and the author identify the authoritative edition, any reviewed translation, and the automatic language options appropriate for the individual page.
  7. Final approval. The author approves the final original-language text, biographical information, and publication metadata.
  8. Web and archival publication. The commentary is published on the IHS website and, where appropriate, prepared as a stable, citable archival edition.
09 · Proposing a commentary

Begin with the idea

A proposal does not need to be a completed manuscript. A useful initial message should briefly identify:

  • the proposed subject or working title;
  • the central question, argument, story, or contribution;
  • the author’s relationship to the subject;
  • the principal sources or basis of knowledge;
  • the language in which the author prefers to write;
  • whether the author can review an English or other translated edition;
  • any sacred, private, legal, linguistic, or ethical considerations that may affect publication;
  • whether the work has appeared elsewhere.

Please do not send restricted ritual material, confidential records, or unpublished third-party documents in an initial proposal.

Proposals may be written in the contributor’s preferred language.

Proposals may be sent to: senior.curator@irokosociety.org

The publication promise

Publication in Iroko Commentaries is not merely a web post. It is an edited, preserved, and citable contribution to the record.

The author’s language, voice, and authority remain at the center of that record. Translation may widen the circle of readers, but it does not replace the words from which the work began.
