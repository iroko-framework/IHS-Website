The Invitation

The Iroko Historical Society invites scholars, practitioners, archivists, community historians, artists, elders, and independent researchers to contribute signed essays to Iroko Commentaries. We publish writing grounded in sustained inquiry, lived experience, documentary evidence, artistic practice, lineage teaching, or community memory. Academic affiliation is welcome and is not required.

Contributors should write in the language in which they can express their knowledge and argument most fully. English is not the default language of authority here, and fluency in English is not a condition of publication.

Current status of the series

Iroko Commentaries is a commissioned series. Most essays begin with an invitation from the editors. Writers whose work falls within the scope of the series are nonetheless welcome to send a brief proposal at any time.

IHS reads every proposal but does not guarantee a reply, editorial review, or publication, and does not accept unsolicited completed manuscripts.

Scope

What a commentary is

An Iroko Commentary is a signed, edited essay that interprets, questions, documents, or reflects upon the histories, religions, cultures, archives, and living communities within the scope of the Society. It may advance an argument, preserve a perspective, examine an artifact, reconsider an inherited interpretation, or place lived religious knowledge into conversation with the historical record.

A commentary does not require the apparatus of a peer-reviewed journal article. It does require intellectual seriousness, responsible representation, and a clear basis of knowledge. A signed commentary represents the considered perspective of its author. It is not an institutional statement and not a declaration of universally accepted doctrine.

Contributors

Who we publish

Practitioners and lineage authorities

Initiated practitioners, tradition bearers, and elders writing from within a named lineage or community.

Scholars and scholar-practitioners

Researchers working on Afro-Atlantic religion, history, art, law, and material culture, inside or outside the academy.

Archivists and public historians

Archivists, librarians, curators, and oral-history practitioners writing on custody, description, and access.

Artists and cultural workers

Artists, musicians, and community historians whose practice constitutes its own form of research and record.

Initiatory standing may be essential to certain subjects. No credential, office, degree, or title grants universal authority over every lineage, community, or tradition. Contributors should identify the basis and the limits of their perspective.

Editorial standards

What we look for

  • A clear contribution A discernible question, argument, interpretation, recollection, or documentary intervention. Not a general opinion.
  • Situated authority An account, direct or through the substance of the essay, of how the author knows what they know, placed within an identifiable period, region, community, lineage, archive, or body of evidence.
  • Responsible evidence Historical and factual claims supported where support is appropriate. Citations may follow Chicago style or another clear format agreed with the editors.
  • Respect without avoidance Commentaries may be critical, revisionist, or contested. Respectful treatment does not require false consensus or the suppression of difficult questions.
  • Care with restricted knowledge Particular caution regarding sacred, restricted, private, initiatory, or community-controlled material. The guidelines set out what we will not publish.

Process

From proposal to publication

  1. Proposal The author is invited to write, or sends a brief proposal describing the subject, the contribution, and the basis of their knowledge. A completed manuscript is not needed and not wanted at this stage.
  2. Editorial fit The editors consider whether the proposed work serves the historical, cultural, scholarly, or community purpose of the series, and respond only if it does.
  3. Draft and review The editors read the manuscript for structure, clarity, evidence, and the treatment of sacred or contested material. The author retains control over the substance of the argument.
  4. Editions and approval Author and editors identify the authoritative edition, any reviewed translation, and the final biographical note and publication metadata.
  5. Publication The commentary is published on the IHS website and, where appropriate, prepared as a stable archival edition with a persistent identifier naming the author as creator.
Publication in Iroko Commentaries is not a web post. It is an edited, preserved, and citable contribution to the record. The author's language, voice, and authority remain at the center of that record. Translation may widen the circle of readers. It does not replace the words from which the work began.
